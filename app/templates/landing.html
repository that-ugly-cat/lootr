A funding table that watches the horizon by itself

Public calls, prizes, accelerators, subsidised loans and investors are scattered across dozens of pages that change without telling anyone. Lootr keeps them on one table, checks them on a schedule, and files what it finds in a queue a human approves. Nothing enters the table on its own.

1
It knows the company Legal form, size, locations, qualifications, team and product lines with their own maturity. Every judgement is made against that profile, not in the abstract.
2
It reads the fine print Who is eligible, how much of the cost is covered, whether there is an advance, and which cumulative ceilings the money counts against.
3
A human decides Discovery proposes, people approve. Decided proposals are never deleted: they are the record of what changed, when, and why.
plain words What does it actually do?

Somebody has to keep track of every way a company can be funded. Grant pages, competition deadlines, accelerator batches, the investors worth talking to. It is a job nobody has time for, and the cost of missing one is measured in months.

Lootr keeps that list. It goes out on a schedule, looks at the sources it has been told to watch, and comes back with what is new and what has changed. It does not edit the list itself: it writes a proposal, shows what it would change and why, and waits. Approving is always a human action.

Because it holds a profile of the company, it can say something more useful than "here is a grant". It can say which product line the call fits, whether the size and age of the company qualify, and how much of a spending ceiling the money would eat.

geek mode How is it built?

FastAPI and SQLite, HTMX and Jinja2 for the interface, JWT cookies for auth. Discovery runs inside the app: a nightly link monitor with no model in the loop, a per-source semantic scan using Claude with server-side web search and a strict tool that validates the proposal payload, and an evaluator that scores eligibility and fit for each pair of opportunity and product line.

The schema is deliberately domain-neutral. Anything sector- or jurisdiction-specific — cumulative ceilings, legal qualifications, tag vocabularies, the source list — is data, not columns. Derived facts such as company age, team composition and remaining headroom are computed on read, so they cannot go stale.

There is an MCP surface at /mcp behind a revocable capability URL, and a compact REST dump at /ono, so an assistant can read the whole model and file proposals into the same queue as the scanner.
